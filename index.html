Keep Your Browser From Becoming a To-Do List

A browser is useful for finding information, but it becomes stressful when every open tab starts to feel like a task you have not finished.

Separate now from later

Open tabs often mix several kinds of work together: something you need today, something you may read later, a page you want to remember, and a task you are avoiding. The first step is to separate these categories. Keep only the tabs connected to the task in front of you. Save the rest in a note, reading list, or resource page that you review later.

This small distinction makes it easier to see what deserves your attention. A tab is no longer a vague reminder. It either supports the work you are doing now or it has a clear place to wait.

Give recurring resources a home

Some pages do not belong in a project folder because you use them again and again. Calendar pages, cloud folders, writing tools, and trusted references are easier to manage in a compact 링크모음. Keep the collection limited to pages that are genuinely useful in your routine.

When a recurring resource has a reliable home, you can close its tab without worrying that it will disappear into a long bookmark list.

Use a closing habit

Before you stop work, take two minutes to close unrelated tabs. Save the pages that support an active project, remove pages that no longer matter, and leave only the next useful step visible. This gives your next work session a calmer beginning.

Make fewer promises to yourself

Every open tab makes a small promise: read this, compare that, return here, decide later. Reducing tabs is not about becoming more strict. It is about making promises you can realistically keep. Save what matters, close what does not, and let your browser return to being a tool rather than a list of unfinished work.
